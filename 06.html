1. Executive Summary

Decision priority: Improve driver-side fulfillment during the 16:00–20:00 demand window, beginning at 18:00–19:00, while diagnosing elevated-risk pickup locations led by Khan Market and Akshardham.
  • Fulfillment loss is substantial: 16,934 of 44,633 retained bookings did not complete, representing 37.9% of booking attempts.
  • Driver-side reliability is the largest lever: driver cancellations caused 7,966 failures, or 47.0% of all unsuccessful bookings. Together with 3,163 no-driver-found cases, driver and supply outcomes represented 65.7% of all failures.
  • Evening is the greatest customer-impact window: 16:00–20:00 generated 6,045 unsuccessful bookings. At 18:00, 1,404 of 3,659 bookings failed; at 19:00, 1,276 of 3,317 failed.
  • Location risk is uneven: Khan Market had 123 failures from 253 bookings, a 48.6% failure level; Akshardham had 117 from 242, or 48.3%. Both materially exceed the 37.9% network level.
  • Vehicle evidence is directional rather than complete: across the four vehicle types visible in the supplied comparison, unsuccessful-booking levels range from 37.3% for Go Mini to 38.4% for Go Sedan. A complete comparison for the critical 18:00–19:00 period is not available.

2. Business Context

Booking failures affect customer trust and reduce the share of demand that converts into completed rides. The evidence supports a network-level prioritization decision: focus first on failure modes with the greatest number of affected customers, then target local operating conditions where risk and meaningful booking activity coincide.

The analysis covers retained bookings dated from 1 January 2024 through 30 December 2024. It addresses overall failure burden, timing, pickup-location patterns, selected vehicle comparisons, and recorded reasons for applicable failure outcomes. It does not establish what operational condition caused any particular failure.

3. Analysis Approach

The assessment compares completed and unsuccessful bookings across final outcomes, booking hour, pickup location, location-hour combinations, and the vehicle types visible in the supplied results. Priorities are based on both the number of affected bookings and the likelihood that a booking fails, so isolated high-risk pockets are not treated as equivalent to large-scale customer exposure.

Interpretation limits: 107 duplicate records and 65 bookings with conflicting outcomes were excluded from the retained booking population. Reasons are available for driver cancellations, customer cancellations, and incomplete rides, but not for no-driver-found cases. Detailed failure composition by priority location, hour, and vehicle type was not completed, and the supplied vehicle preview does not support a complete cross-vehicle assessment during 18:00–19:00.

5. Implications

The evidence supports a two-level response. At network level, driver cancellations and no-driver-found cases are the biggest opportunity because they account for nearly two-thirds of all failures. At local level, the early-evening demand peak and locations such as Khan Market and Akshardham should be prioritized because they combine material customer impact with elevated failure exposure. Customer-cancellation records also contain signs of address and pickup coordination issues, including drivers asking customers to cancel or not moving toward the pickup location; these are signals for review rather than proof of underlying causes. Incomplete rides require separate attention because vehicle breakdown is one of the leading recorded reasons.

6. Recommendations

  • First: Concentrate supply and dispatch actions in the 16:00–20:00 window, with immediate attention to 18:00–19:00. Track reductions in driver cancellations and no-driver-found outcomes as the primary success measures.
  • Second: Launch focused operating reviews at Khan Market and Akshardham, then assess Nehru Place, Shivaji Park, and Saket based on their combined failure burden and booking activity. Use high-rate, low-volume location-hour cells as investigation prompts rather than standalone allocation targets.
  • Third: Review driver-cancellation controls and support around the leading reported categories, including personal and vehicle-related issues, customer-related issues, and passenger-count constraints.
  • Fourth: Improve pickup and booking coordination by addressing wrong-address reports, customer reports that drivers asked them to cancel, and reports of drivers not moving toward pickup locations.
  • Fifth: Strengthen vehicle-readiness follow-up for incomplete rides, given the prominence of vehicle breakdown among recorded reasons.
  • Next evidence required: produce complete outcome comparisons for peak versus non-peak hours, month-by-month location persistence, vehicle-type performance at 18:00–19:00, and reason patterns by priority location and vehicle type. This would distinguish targeted operational issues from broader network reliability gaps.
